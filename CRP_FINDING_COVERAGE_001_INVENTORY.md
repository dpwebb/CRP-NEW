## 4. Month-only finding limitation (precision vs exception vs missing implementation)

Month-only anchors are compared as a whole-month range (GAP-INGEST-006), never a substituted day. Three
distinct limitations govern findings, and they are not interchangeable:

1. **Precision restriction (extraction):** public-record legal-event facts — judgment entry, tax lien paid,
   bankruptcy order-for-relief/adjudication — are accepted only at DAY precision
   (`acceptLegalEventFact` filters `precision === 'DAY'`). A month-only public-record date never reaches the
   anchor, so FCRA-605A-1/2/3, US-NY ×3 and US-CA cannot age from a month-only event.
2. **Exception gate:** FCRA-605A-1/3/4/5 and US-NY ×3 record report-unobservable §1681c(b)/§380-j(f)(2) use
   exceptions that stay `resolved: false`, so `breach_established` is false and no finding is emitted
   regardless of precision. This is the GAP-FINDING-001 exception-evaluation constraint.
3. **Missing implementation:** CA and GB have no finding-enabled rule at all (not a precision or exception
   issue — an absent admitted rule).

The AU account/adverse rules are MONTH-capable at the classifier and can emit VIOLATION from a wholly-before
month.

## 5. Rule-material questions that could unlock findings (not implemented)

No rule-material question is currently admitted, so no clarification question is generated. The off-report
dependencies that a precisely framed consumer question could legitimately resolve, subject to owner admission:

- **Report use** (§1681c(b) and §380-j(f)(2)): "What was this report used for?" — credit transaction
  ≥$150k / life-insurance underwriting ≥$150k / employment ≥$75k / other. Consumer-knowable, off-report, and
  would resolve the use exception, unlocking seven finding-enabled US rules across 57 regions. Must be recorded
  as a separately-sourced consumer assertion, never a bureau-reported fact, with "I don't know" leaving the
  exception unresolved (unknown never becomes false).
- **Earlier bankruptcy** (CA-NS second-bankruptcy): an off-report completeness fact (a distinct prior event)
  that a single discharge date cannot establish; a consumer assertion would remain a consumer statement and
  would not, by itself, establish a second bankruptcy event identity.

Questions must not cure mandatory report omissions; none of these is a report-reading question.
