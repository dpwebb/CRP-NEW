Credit Regulator Pro (CRP) helps you review credit reports, understand supported reporting issues, and prepare correction or verification packets that you choose to send. This policy explains how we handle your information.

## Information we collect

- Account details, including your email, protected sign-in credentials, and contact details you choose to save.
- Credit reports, screenshots, identification, proof of address, and other documents you upload. These can include sensitive information about you and other people named in a report.
- Case details, including extracted report facts, your answers, results, report comparisons, issue selections, packet edits and approvals.
- Purchase and support details, including plan and access status, provider transaction identifiers, refunds, cancellations, and support requests. The payment provider handles payment details under its own notice; CRP does not need your full card number to confirm access.

## Storage and deletion

Your uploaded report and case results remain in the active service until you delete the case or account; reports have no automatic expiry setting. Account-level identification, address, and supporting documents remain until you remove them or delete the account. Deleting a case does not remove documents saved separately in your account. You can review your stored case and document inventory in the service.

Deleting a case removes its active report files and results. Deleting an account removes active account contact details, cases, stored files, results, purchase records, and sessions. **Active-service deletion does not establish immediate erasure from separate backups.** Payment records held by the payment provider follow its practices and applicable retention duties. We use access controls and safeguards suited to sensitive report information. No online service can guarantee absolute security. Contact us if you believe your account or data has been compromised.

Legal obligations and other people's rights may limit a request. You may also contact your privacy regulator if you disagree with our response.
