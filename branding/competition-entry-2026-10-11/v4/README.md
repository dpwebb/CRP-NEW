# Credit Regulator Pro — Evidence Link

The final logo is a full-name wordmark paired with an open, connected symbol. Its two opposing boundaries meet through one visible horizontal line: a restrained visual shorthand for linking a specific report fact to a review. The mark makes no claim that a dispute will change a credit score or produce a legal outcome.

The wordmark is outlined from PT Sans Bold (SIL Open Font License 1.1); final SVGs require no installed fonts. Source Sans Pro appears only in the presentation board labels. Navy `#142B3A` carries the identity; copper `#A57543` marks the structural link. On dark surfaces, the copper is lightened to `#DDB787`. Keep the complete name with the symbol in new-customer communications. Use the symbol alone in tiny UI spaces after the name is established nearby.

## Research applied

- A [2021 multi-study logo paper](https://digitalcommons.usf.edu/mai_facpub/8/) found less familiar brands generally benefited from text-led rather than image-led logos. This motivated the prominent, legible full name.
- [2025 experiments on logo simplicity](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0148296325004035) associated simpler marks with perceived competence, while noting a warmth tradeoff. The symbol uses a small number of aligned elements; the humanist lettering and warm accent soften the system.
- [Research on structured visual communications](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/00222429221142281) found better fit for utilitarian brands, motivating exact alignments and repeatable spacing.
- [2024 research on logo color and background](https://academic.oup.com/jcr/article-abstract/51/4/820/7633736) shows that color meanings depend on context. The white primary and dark reverse are deliberate applications, not a claim that navy universally creates trust.

These studies inform design choices; none establishes that this logo will cause subscriptions, create lasting memory, or make a viewer feel empowered. Those outcomes require target-customer testing and a product experience that actually preserves consumer choice. Trademark clearance is also needed before exclusive adoption.
